How NeoGuard works

  1. A CTG machine records the baby’s heartbeat and the mother’s contractions during labour. It prints a trace like the one at the top of this page.
  2. The CTG software summarises the trace into numbers: the average heart rate, how much it varies, and how many times it speeds up or drops.
  3. You type those numbers in. The model learned from 2,126 real traces that three expert doctors had already labelled Normal, Suspect or Pathological.
  4. It compares your trace with those cases, shows how likely each label is, which readings drove the answer, and what to do next.
Minutes matterFetal distress can worsen quickly in labour. A fast first read helps the right case reach a doctor sooner.
Experts are scarceTrained CTG readers are concentrated in large hospitals. Peripheral clinics often have the machine but not the reader.
Explained, not a black boxEvery result shows which readings pushed it, so staff can learn from it and challenge it.
NeoGuard is decision support, not a diagnosis, and never replaces clinical judgement or local protocol. The model learned from 2,126 CTGs recorded in Porto, Portugal and labelled by three obstetricians (UCI Cardiotocography dataset). It has not been clinically validated in other settings. The triage thresholds are deliberately cautious, so some normal traces will be flagged as suspect.
